Second Voice

A coercion-aware payment interruptor for UPI. It sits in the payer bank's authorization hook, scores whether the account holder is being manipulated right now, and answers with a conversation instead of a banner. The receiving bank runs the same stack on its own accounts and shares one word back: a receiver risk band.

Video 3:30, limit 3 to 4 min Cast 3 people Screens phone + two bank consoles Thesis "Is she being manipulated?" not "Is this her?"

Demo arc

One victim, one scam script, one arc from phone call to held transfer, the counter-example that shows a normal payment flows untouched, then Bank B trapping the money even when the payer-side hold is overridden. The presenter narrates. One teammate holds the phone and plays Sunita. One teammate, off camera, voices the scammer and reads the real digital arrest script.

  1. 0:00 – 0:20

    Hook

    "Every bank fraud system asks one question: is this really the account holder? In the fastest growing fraud in India, the answer is yes. She is on her own phone, typing her own PIN, because a man calling himself a CBI officer told her to."

    On screen: title card, one number from I4C on digital arrest losses, sourced on the slide. Nothing else.

  2. 0:20 – 0:50

    Set the scene

    Sunita Rao, 58, retired teacher in Pune, 11 years with the bank. A video call is already running. The scammer voice reads lines from the actual script: her Aadhaar was used for money laundering, a warrant exists, she must stay on the line, she must not tell her family, she must transfer her savings to a "verification account" and will get it back after clearance.

    On screen: split view. Left, the mock bank app on the phone, mirrored. Right, the fraud-ops console, empty, with the live event stream ticking at zero.

  3. 0:50 – 1:20

    The attack

    Sunita adds a new payee, "Rakesh Kumar", types ₹2,40,000, taps Pay. The console lights up as the request hits the scoring hook. The presenter reads the signals as they appear.

    payee added 2 min ago amount 11× her 90-day max call active 23 min screen-share app installed 40 min ago payee first seen on our book yesterday 12 of our customers paid it today, all first-time Bank B receiver band: HIGH registry hit: payee mobile flagged by DoT FRI (stubbed)

    "Any one of these is noise. A pensioner adding a new payee happens every day. All eight together have a name. The system's best guess is digital arrest, with a score of 0.91. Note what we never see: the receiver's account at Bank B. Bank B's own model saw it, and told us exactly one word: High."

    On screen: the score card flips to HOLD. The phone shows a spinner, then the conversation screen.

  4. 1:20 – 2:00

    The intervention

    The agent speaks English in the prototype. Language is a field on the customer record and a line in the prompt, and the deck shows one Hindi turn to make the point. Its questions come from the digital arrest script, not from a generic template. The scammer voice tries to coach her ("tell them it is for a family emergency") and cannot keep up with the questions.

    Sunita, before this goes through: is someone on a call with you right now who has asked you not to disconnect, or not to tell anyone about this payment?
    [ Yes ] [ No, I know this person ]
    Yes. A CBI officer. He says my Aadhaar was misused and this is a verification account.
    No police, CBI, or court collects money through UPI, and "digital arrest" is not a real legal process. This looks like a scam that has targeted many people this month. I have paused the payment. Would you like me to call your daughter Priya now, on this screen, so she can hear this too?
    [ Call Priya ] [ Talk to the bank ] [ I understand, cancel the payment ]

    Sunita taps "Call Priya". The transfer is placed on a 24-hour hold. The scammer voice goes quiet and hangs up.

    "A banner says 'beware of fraud' and gets dismissed in two seconds under coercion. A question the scammer did not script for breaks the spell. That is the product."

  5. 2:00 – 2:20

    What Bank A sees, and the false-positive story

    Cut to Bank A's ops console. A case has been created with the signal breakdown, the full transcript, and a two-line summary written by the agent. Rakesh Kumar's VPA goes on the bank's payee blocklist and a report is queued to I4C.

    Then the counter-example. Same Sunita, same phone, ₹2,400 to her regular milk vendor. The hook scores it in under 200 ms, tier Low, the payment completes with no interruption at all.

    On screen: case view, then a side-by-side of the two transactions: eight warm signals versus zero.

  6. 2:20 – 2:45

    Bank B, and the override that still fails

    Cut to Bank B's console. Rakesh Kumar's account scored High this morning, before Sunita ever tapped Pay: opened nine days ago, twelve first-time inflows today, each swept out within twenty minutes. That band is what Bank A received during address validation, in the same lookup that returns the payee's name today.

    Then the what-if. The presenter replays Sunita's payment with the hold overridden. The debit goes through. Bank B's hook catches the credit on arrival and holds it for 24 hours. The money is still inside the banking system, not in an ATM in another state.

    "Two banks, two chances. The payer bank stops the person. The receiver bank traps the money. And every bank that joins makes every other bank's score better."

    On screen: Bank B account view with the band, then the held credit with its 24-hour timer.

Decision tiers

The tiering is the answer to "won't this annoy everyone?". Low risk never sees the product. Medium costs a few seconds. Only the pattern that looks like a scam gets a hold.

Receiver bands, shared by Bank B

Bank B scores its own accounts continuously and shares only the band, never account data. The band is one input to Bank A's payer-side score, not a decision on its own.

Architecture

The payer-side hook runs before the debit. The only UPI leg the product touches is the existing address-validation lookup, which Bank B answers with one extra field, the receiver band. Solid boxes are built on Google Cloud for the hackathon. Dashed boxes are simulated for the demo and named as such in the pitch. One constraint shapes the data lane: for a transfer to another bank, the payer bank never sees the receiver's account, so payee reputation is built from the bank's own transaction book plus shared registries, not from receiver account data.

1App sends the pay request. The SDK attaches device context in the same call.
2Payment API publishes the event, including the receiver band from step 9, and waits on the hook with a hard 200 ms budget.
3Scorer pulls the customer's 90-day history and the payee's fan-in across the bank's own customers from BigQuery, queries the registry stub (I4C Suspect Registry, DoT Fraud Risk Indicator), then scores with a weighted model inside the service. Gemini names the suspected script from the signal pattern.
4Medium and above hand off to the Gemini agent with the suspected script attached. Low returns allow immediately.
5The agent grounds its questions in the matching scam script and current RBI advisories.
6Conversation streams to the phone through the Payment API. Each answer re-scores. Hold, cancel, or trusted-contact call.
7Decision, transcript and signals persist to Firestore. Live listeners update the phone and the ops console without polling.
8Ops marks the outcome. It flows back to BigQuery as training labels; confirmed scam payees go on the bank's blocklist for every customer and are reported to I4C.
9Runs first, when the payee is resolved. Bank A asks Bank B to validate the VPA, as every UPI app does today for the name. Bank B answers with the name and a risk band. In the demo this is a direct HTTP call standing in for the NPCI leg.
10Bank B's Payment API asks its receiver scorer for the current band on the account, and later tells it when a credit arrives so a High-band hold can be applied.
11Receiver scorer reads the account's age, KYC depth, first-time inflow fan-in and sweep-out speed from Bank B's BigQuery, scores with the same service using receiver features.
12High-band accounts surface in Bank B's ops console with a 24-hour inflow hold, account review and I4C report.

Build split for the prototype

The submission asks how the build uses Firebase, Firestore, Cloud Run and Gemini, and the prototype has to stay up and clickable for the whole evaluation window. That rules out anything always-on and expensive, so there is no custom Vertex AI endpoint; the coercion score is a weighted model inside the Cloud Run service and Gemini does the reasoning.

Must ship

  • Cloud Run: two services from one codebase, bank-a and bank-b. Payment API, scorer, Gemini agent, address-validation and credit endpoints. Keep one minimum instance each so a judge never hits a cold start.
  • Firestore: customers, payees, 90-day history, Bank B accounts, cases, holds and conversation turns. Live listeners drive the phone screen and both consoles. Seed script in the repo.
  • Firebase Hosting + Auth: the mock bank app and the two ops consoles as static web apps. Consoles behind a demo login whose credentials are printed on the landing page for judges.
  • Gemini on Vertex AI: names the scam script from the signal pattern, runs the intervention conversation with a system prompt per script, writes the case summary. Scam scripts and RBI advisories live in the prompt context for the prototype.
  • Bank B as the second Cloud Run deployment with receiver features, a seeded set of synthetic mule and normal accounts, and the band-sharing endpoint.

If time allows

  • BigQuery as the analytics copy of history and outcomes, with the fan-in query shown live in the video.
  • Pub/Sub for payment.initiated feeding the console event stream and case.resolved feeding BigQuery. The hook itself stays a synchronous call.
  • Vertex AI Search over the scam script corpus, replacing the in-prompt scripts.
  • Hindi conversation turns, with English shown alongside. Not in the prototype; one screenshot in the deck.

Simulated, and said out loud

  • Device signals. The mock app has toggles for active call, remote-access app, and payee age. In production the SDK reads these with Android permissions; iOS loses the call signal.
  • Registry lookups. I4C Suspect Registry and DoT Fraud Risk Indicator are stubbed with their real request and response shapes. A payer bank never sees the receiver's account for a transfer to another bank, so fan-in on our own book plus these registries are the only receiver-side signals available. Same-bank transfers get the full receiver record.
  • Trusted-contact call. A phone on stage rings via a webhook, not a telephony integration.
  • UPI rails. Nothing is sent to NPCI. The Payment API prints "sent to NPCI" on allow, and the credit to Bank B is a direct call between the two deployments.
  • Receiver band in address validation. Bank A calls Bank B directly over HTTP standing in for the NPCI name-lookup leg. The extra band field is a proposal, not a live NPCI specification, and the pitch says so.
  • Labelled data. The launch model leans on script matching and heuristics; the retraining loop is shown, not yet proven.

Description draft (1,001 characters)

Second Voice is a coercion-aware payment interruptor for UPI. Fraud systems ask: is this the account holder? In digital-arrest and investment scams the answer is yes: the victim authorises the payment under coercion, so rules see nothing. Our hook runs in the payer bank's authorisation path before the debit. It combines payer-side signals (new payee, amount vs 90-day history, active call, remote-access app) with payee reputation from the bank's own book and a risk band shared by the receiving bank. Gemini matches the pattern to a known scam script and runs an adaptive intervention conversation, not a banner, offering a hold and a trusted-contact call. The receiving bank runs the same stack to score its own accounts and hold suspicious inflows. Cloud Run hosts the payment APIs, scorers and Gemini agent for both banks. Firestore stores cases, holds and conversation state, with live listeners driving the phone UI and ops consoles. Firebase Hosting and Auth serve the demo app and consoles.
